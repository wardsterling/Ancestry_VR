    How these numbers are counted
    The grand total counts each archive profile once after evidence-based merges. Same-name records under review stay separate. Family groups are distinct cited parent sets with children, deduplicated across reports; a one-parent set and a two-parent set remain separate. Family listings are distinguished from parentage. A selected person can belong to several groups; the selection counts groups containing at least one selected person.
    States come from explicitly recorded birth or death places. Town-only and unresolved places are grouped separately. Generations are relative to each report. Report, generation, family and statement categories can overlap, so their counts must not be added together. Every percentage uses the unique people in the current selection.
    Education, health, occupation, residence and military counts use attributed statements extracted from source text. Gender is only categorized when explicitly labeled in a source; names and pronouns are not used to infer it. “Not recorded” means no supported value was extracted, not that the person lacked an education or condition. “Details hidden” follows the living-person switch. Original reports may contain errors; open the evidence before drawing conclusions.
